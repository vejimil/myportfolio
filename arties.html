01 The problem

Open calls for artists are scattered across each foundation's own website, and the central portal is only where you submit. Every call has different eligibility — where you're registered as a resident, years of practice, past selections — and writing an artist statement is hard. Meanwhile, the 2024 Korean artist survey puts average annual income in fine art at about 6 million won, so paid help is out of reach for most.

Existing boards list notices but don't check them against you, and statement feedback is sold as a human service. Nothing tied discovery, eligibility, deadlines, and feedback into one flow.

02 One flow, four steps

  1. CollectNotices are gathered by hand; AI drafts a structured summary and a person reviews it before publishing.
  2. MatchA short profile is compared with each call's requirements.
  3. RemindSaved calls are sorted by deadline so nothing slips by.
  4. ReviewPaste a draft and get questions about it, tied to the call's criteria.

Calls can be filtered by field and by what you'd receive — grants, studio space, exhibition opportunities, awards, mentoring, or paid work — and sorted by deadline, fit with your profile, newest, or amount.

03 Designing inside legal limits

Before writing UI, I researched where a service like this crosses legal lines, and turned the answers into design rules:

  • Compare, don't judge. Eligibility shows as "requirement met / needs checking / not met" and "M of N required conditions met" — never "you can apply" or a chance of selection.
  • Humans verify the facts. Deadlines and eligibility text are checked by a person, shown with a link to the original notice and the date last confirmed. The service's own commentary is visually separated from the original.
  • Questions, not answers. The AI review asks things like "Which sentence answers criterion 2?" and never produces text you could paste straight into an application. Every AI output is labelled.

04 A Mondrian system with one job for colour

The interface uses white planes, thick black lines, and the three primaries — but colour has exactly one meaning: how close the deadline is. Red is within 7 days, yellow within 21, blue beyond. Eligibility and review status never use colour; they use ink text and ✓ ? ✕ icons, so the two signals can't be confused.

05 Why I'm building it

It sits exactly where my studies meet my making: aesthetics gives me a way to design what good feedback on an artist statement looks like, and building it myself means those rules end up in the product instead of in a slide deck.

What I'm learning

Deciding what a product must not do is as much design as deciding what it does. The limits shaped the flow, the wording, and even the colour system.
